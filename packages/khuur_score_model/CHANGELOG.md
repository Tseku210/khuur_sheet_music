## 0.5.0
* **Breaking:** `Transposition.apply` takes an `Alterations`, the key plus the accidentals read so far in the bar, in place of a `KeySignature`. A caller that passed a key writes `apply(pitch, Alterations(key))`.
* **Breaking:** `Transposition.diatonic` moves a note by staff positions onto the accidental in force at its new position, as MuseScore 4 does. That is the key's, or the one an earlier note of the bar left there, read as the edit leaves it. In F major the bar F♯4 B4 A4 E4 steps up to G4 C5 B♭4 F4, where it went to G♯4 C♯5 B♭4 F4. `diatonic(7)` and `diatonic(-7)` no longer keep an accidental, so an octave move uses `Transposition.interval(Interval.octave)`.
* `Transpose` by staff steps sets each moved note's `AccidentalRequest` back to `auto`, clears the accidentals of a moved chord's ornament, and gives a chord symbol's root the key's alteration. A step over a chord with two notes on one letter in the same octave, such as F4 and F♯4, is refused with `InvalidValue`, since both would land on one note.
* `Transpose(selection, by, keys: true)` also moves the key of each bar the range holds whole, by `Transposition.interval` or `Transposition.chromatic`, and spells those bars in the new key, as one undo step. A key past seven sharps or flats is written as the key that sounds the same. A range that misses a staff that reads a key, shown or hidden, moves its notes and no key. The bars after the range keep their key.
* `PlaybackCompiler` times grace notes as MuseScore 4 plays them. An acciaccatura takes a 64th rather than a 32nd, so a slow tempo no longer stretches it into a note of its own. Several graces before one chord, slashed or not, take a 64th each, where any appoggiatura among them used to make them all take half the chord, which played a trill's closing notes as a slow triplet. A lone appoggiatura takes its written value, up to half its chord, or two thirds of one longer than an eighth in a compound meter, where it always took half. Graces still play on the beat, and together never take more than half their chord, except that lone appoggiatura.
* `PlaybackCompiler` plays graces right after a trilled chord in their voice as the end of its trill. They play quick at the end of the trilled chord, a 64th each and together at most half of it, and the chord they lead to plays on its beat. The trill ends on its main note before them, or on the note above when they start on its main note, so that note does not strike twice. This holds for a trill ornament and a trill line, and for graces that open the next bar played. Play that starts at the graced chord plays its graces on it, as before. A lone appoggiatura keeps its own beat and leans on its chord, since it is written to. So do graces after a trilled chord that ties on, after a rest, and after a chord with another ornament, such as a mordent under a trill line.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` no longer refuses a note whose `<duration>` disagrees with its `<type>` and dots. Outside a tuplet the note keeps its written value, as in MuseScore. A `<backup>` after such a note counts from where the durations put the cursor, so a second voice starts on time. A `<backup>` past the start of its bar stops there, as in MuseScore, instead of refusing the file.
* A tie with no stop ends on the note of the same pitch in the next event of its voice.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` reads a direction's `<offset>`. With `sound="yes"` the mark moves by the offset. Without it the offset only moves where the mark is drawn, so a dynamic or words go to the nearest note or rest of their staff, or stay at the bar's end or where they are written when that is nearer. The exception is a dynamic or words written where no note or rest of their staff starts. They keep the offset's time when a note or rest of another staff of the part starts there, since MuseScore 3 writes such a mark at the end of the bar with an offset back to its time. So the `p` in bar 88 of the Moonlight sonata's last movement plays after the downbeat `f`, not on it. A tempo mark or a rehearsal mark goes to the nearest note or rest of any staff of the part. A hairpin, dashed line or pedal line end moves by the offset unless where it is written is nearer than any note or rest. An octave line stays where it is written, even with `sound="yes"`.
* A trill mark whose wavy line never stops is kept as a trill.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` keeps a dynamic, words, a chord symbol, a tempo mark or a rehearsal mark written at the end of the last bar, or moved past it by an `<offset>`. It sits where the bar's last event starts. Before, it was dropped.
* `musicXmlFromBytes` decodes a UTF-16 document about four times faster.
* `PlaybackCompiler` sounds a note that has no staccato, staccatissimo or tenuto and is not slurred for 0.99 of its length, the share MuseScore 4 gives a string, a wind or a voice. It let the note go after 0.9, so a whole note at 60 quarters a minute left 400 ms of silence before the next.
* Each note of a trill, a mordent, a turn or a tremolo sounds until the next one starts, and the marks on the chord set the length of the last. Each one used to stop early, which a bowed string plays as a gap on every note.
* A trill plays about 13 notes a second at any tempo, where it alternated in 32nds and so played 5 notes a second at 40 quarters a minute and 27 at 200. A fermata or a rit. over it adds notes. A short trill plays an odd count of notes to end on its main note and an even count to end on the note above, at 8 to 27 notes a second, so a trilled eighth at 120 quarters a minute plays four notes. A note too short for that plays alone. Mordents and turns still play in 32nds.
* A hairpin arrives at the next dynamic only when that dynamic lies the way it moves, louder for a crescendo and softer for a diminuendo, as in MuseScore 4. Otherwise it moves one level and the dynamic takes over, so a crescendo before a subito piano grows and then drops, where it used to play as a diminuendo.
* A compile looks up each chord's slurs and trill lines by binary search, where it scanned them all. Beethoven's Op. 132 compiles in about a third of the time, and a score with thousands of slurs no longer pauses play. The compiled script is unchanged.
* Playback and MIDI export play a `Glissando` as chromatic steps, as MuseScore 4 plays a chromatic glissando, where it played two plain notes. The note a line starts on moves a semitone at a time to the note it ends on, in equal steps over its chord's time. The other notes of the chord hold, as in MuseScore 4, and a chord with two lines glides each note along its own, so crossed lines glide the right way. A note that ties on plays its tie and does not glide.
* MusicXML import reads a `<slide>` as a glissando, as MuseScore 4 does. Export writes it back as a `<glissando>`.
* **Breaking:** A `Glissando` joins two notes, not two chords. `Glissando(from:, to:)` names them by `NoteId`, and `Glissando.notesOn` and `Score.glissandoNotes` give the notes it joins. A named end is on that note in whichever voice of the staff plays it, so a line of voice two can end on a note of voice one. A start that names no note is on the top note of the event its voice has there. `Glissando.notesOn` takes the staff's `Instrument` and counts a chord's notes by staff line, then by sound, as MuseScore 4 orders them. A drum's line is where its instrument prints the sound. A last end that names no note is on the note of its chord at the place the start note has in its own chord, counted from the lowest line, or on the top note of a chord with fewer notes, as MuseScore 4 picks the end of a new glissando. So a line from a single note ends on the note on the lowest line of a chord. `AddSpanner` and each edit name the note picked, so adding or removing another note of either chord later does not move the end, as MuseScore 4 keeps the note it picked when the line was made. Where its voice has a gap, an end that names no note is on voice one's event. A last end in that gap is instead on the first chord another voice starts there, when there is one, if voice one has held its event since the line's first event or before. An edit that moves a named note moves that end to where the note starts. One that shortens the chord a line starts on removes the line, except `Paste`, which keeps it. One that shortens the chord it ends on keeps the line while that chord keeps its start, unless it is a note or rest entered with a ripple. One that takes a named note off the staff removes the line, as MuseScore 4 does, and undo brings both back. `Score.anchorOf` gives the event each end is on. `AddSpanner` refuses a `Glissando` naming a note that no voice of the staff plays at that end, with `StaleReference`. It refuses a `Glissando` whose end is on an event that starts no later than the event its start is on, with `InvalidValue`, whether or not it names a note. An edit that leaves such a line removes it, and undo brings it back. MusicXML import leaves it out. A JSON file whose glissando names a note and ends that way does not load. One that names no note, as a file saved before 0.5.0 can hold, loads without the line.
* A new `Glissando` from `AddSpanner` that starts on a note, names no last note and would end on a rest ends instead on the first chord a voice of its staff starts there, in voice order from voice one. `AddSpanner` names that note, and removing the note removes the line. With no chord on its staff there the end stays on the rest. MuseScore 4 picks a glissando's end only when it makes the line, from the first chord of the part there, on any of the part's staves. A line read from a file, or one whose start rest later becomes a note, keeps its end on the rest.
* MusicXML import keeps which note carries a `<glissando>` or `<slide>`, in any voice of the staff, and export writes each mark back on that note. A stop written on another staff names no note, so the line ends on its own staff, where an end that names no note falls. The JSON format saves a glissando's notes as `fromNote` and `toNote`. A file saved without them loads, and a line there glides and draws between the notes an end that names no note is on, where it glided every note. The first edit names those notes. A file whose glissando names a note that no voice of its staff plays at that end loads without that line.
* `Paste` and a measure-repeat sign written out by an edit keep a glissando on the copies of its notes.
* **Breaking:** A slur can no longer end on an event that starts before the event it starts on, such as a note of voice one held from before the slur where the slur's own voice has a gap and no other voice starts a chord. A tied part of a note counts as an event of its own. `AddSpanner` refuses such a slur with `InvalidValue`, an edit that leaves one removes it, MusicXML import leaves it out, and a JSON file loads without it. A slur may still end on a note of voice one that starts with its first note, where its own voice has a gap. A slur that names a voice in `Slur.endVoice` ends on that voice's chord only when the chord starts after the slur's first note.
* The last end of a slur, or of a glissando that names no note there, can lie where its voice has a gap, under an event that voice one has held, through any ties and barlines, since before the line's first event. For a glissando, an event of voice one that starts with the line's first event counts as held too. That end is now on the chord another voice starts there, the first such voice in voice order, when there is one. With 0.4.0 it was on voice one's event, so the line ran backward. `SetMeter`, ripple edits and `Paste` that split voice one's event into tied parts keep the end on its chord. `Score.anchorOf` gives the event each end is on. A start end in a gap is on the event of voice one, as before, and `Score.anchorAt` is unchanged.
* MusicXML import ends a slur on the chord its stop is written on, when that chord is on the same staff, as MuseScore 4 does. A slur whose stop is written on a note of a voice other than voice one ran back to voice one's note. A slur whose stop is written on a note of voice one that started before the slur's start is left out. A slur or glissando whose stop is on another staff is dropped when, at the stop's time, its own staff has no event of the line's voice, no chord of another voice that starts there, and only an event of voice one that started before the line's start. A kept cross-staff slur ends on an event of its own staff.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` keeps a slur whose start is written on a note of voice one on that note when the voice of its stop has a note or rest of its own under it. It reads the slur in voice one with `Slur.endVoice` naming the stop's voice, as MuseScore 4 takes a slur's voice from its start note. Before, the start moved to the other voice's note or rest, or the slur was dropped where voice one held one note under both ends. A glissando whose start is written on a rest of voice one, as `scoreToMusicXml` writes one that starts in a gap of its own voice over that rest, keeps that start too. MuseScore 4 reads no glissando from a mark on a rest.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` reads a slur whose stop is on its own staff in the voice of its start mark, as MuseScore 4 does, when the voice of its stop has a `<forward>` under that mark. `Slur.endVoice` names the stop's voice.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes a `placement` alone on the start of a slur that starts where its own voice is silent and stops in that voice, so the library reads that slur back in its voice. The reader trusts that mark only in a file this library wrote, because MuseScore 4.0 and 4.1 write a placement on every slur.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes a glissando from a note to a rest under a number above the part's glissandi of any other shape, so this library reads it back. Lines of this shape can share a number. MuseScore 4 reads a glissando mark only on a note. It draws no line from such a start, and the number keeps that start from closing on the next glissando. A part that would need a number past MusicXML's limit of 16 leaves that line out, and so does a line whose first note has a let-ring tie, because MuseScore 4 then keeps the start with no end. A line that starts on a rest is still written, for this library to read back. MuseScore 4 reads no line from it.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes a glissando's start before its stop when the line ends on a voice written earlier in the bar. MuseScore 4 ignored such a stop, so the line closed on the wrong note or stayed open. That staff's bar is now written in time order, and other bars are unchanged.
* The stop of a glissando from a rest no longer closes another glissando of its number in MuseScore 4.
* `PlaybackCompiler` slurs from the event a slur starts on to the event it ends on, as `Score.anchorOf` finds them and the sheet draws them.
* **Breaking:** on a part whose instrument has `Instrument.singleNoteDynamics`, a note softer than velocity 84, f included, strikes at velocity 84 times its accents and its level moves to the channel's expression, MIDI controller 11. A tool that reads `PlaybackNote.velocity` as the dynamic sees those notes at 84.
* A held note follows the dynamics while it sounds, as in MuseScore 4. Each `ChannelSetup` has an `Expression`, a list of `ExpressionPoint`s. Under a hairpin it moves in steps of a sixteenth and at least 24, an fp eases from f to p over 0.4 seconds, and an sf, sfz or rfz holds its level until the next note or rest on its staff starts, or the bar ends. From velocity 84 up the expression is full, so a held note does not swell past about f.
* `Instrument.singleNoteDynamics` defaults by General MIDI program, as MuseScore 4's instrument list does in 641 of its 646 instruments. It is on for organs, bowed strings, voices, brass, winds and synths, and off for pianos, mallets, guitars, basses, pizzicato, harp, timpani, other plucked and struck sounds, and kits. A part that shares its channel keeps full expression. JSON stores it only when it differs from the default.
* `scoreToMidi` writes each channel's expression as controller 11, at its start and wherever it changes.
* An `Erase` of many items copies the bar list once for each pass it makes, not once for each item, so its cost grows in step with the score. It copied the list once for each bar it wrote, each chord it took a head from and each tie it cleared. Erasing a chord in every bar of 16000 bars takes 80 ms where it took 770 ms.
* **Breaking:** a rippled `Erase`, and a `SetValue` that shortens a note with `Ripple.part` or `Ripple.staff`, move a line end that falls in the removed time as MuseScore 4 does. A slur end moves to the music after the removed time, so the slur ends on the note that takes the removed note's place, and the slur is dropped when both its ends land on one event. A hairpin, a pedal line or another line ends where the time it covered ends, and is dropped when no time is left. Both used to end on the last onset before the removed time.
* A glissando goes with its notes. A ripple drops a glissando when the note or rest it starts or ends on starts in the removed time, even when part of that note stays. `SetBarLength` drops one when that note starts in the cut. This holds whether or not the line names its notes. A glissando is dropped too when the chord it starts on loses its end or its middle, as in MuseScore 4. One that ends on a chord that loses its end or its middle stays.
* `SetBarLength` moves a slur end in the cut to the music after it, as a ripple does. A slur that ends in the cut of the last bar ends on the last event left in its voice.
* A rippled `Erase` of many chords closes every gap of a section in one pass, so the result no longer depends on the order of the selection. Its cost grows in step with the section. Erasing a chord in every bar of 4000 bars with `Ripple.part` takes 30 ms where it took 47 s, and 35 ms where it took 17 s when the score has a new section every eight bars. A score of many sections no longer copies its bar list for each section. Erasing a chord in every bar of 64000 bars with a new section every eight bars takes 0.5 s, and its cost now grows in step with the score. A rippled erase of a range across many sections ripples each section with only the lines that have an end in it. Erasing a range over 16000 bars with a glissando in every bar and a new section every eight bars takes 70 ms where it took 1.5 s. An erase in a score of many sections whose notes are tied no longer reads every event of the score for each section. Erasing a chord in every bar of 4000 such bars takes 55 ms where it took 1.3 s. Both now grow in step with the score.
* A rippled `Erase` writes a note that it shortens once, as the fewest values that fit each bar, where it could leave two tied notes. It keeps the tie into such a note while the note keeps its start, and it no longer refuses with `WouldSplitTuplet` when the tuplet ends up whole inside one bar.
* A bar under a measure-repeat sign loses the sign when a ripple pulls music of its staff back across its end.
* **Breaking:** before every edit, the ends of each slur and glissando are stored where they are, as MuseScore 4 keeps the chords and notes a line was made on. A slur's first end is stored when it is on a chord of the slur's own voice, and its last end when it is on a chord that starts after its first point. Its points move to those chords' onsets. `Slur.endVoice` names the voice of the chord it ends on, and is cleared when that is the slur's own voice. A glissando names the note each end is on. A later edit of other notes no longer moves a stored end, and changing the pitch of an end note keeps the line. A line's stored ends can change after any edit, even one far from it, so a score saved after an edit can differ from the one loaded. A glissando loaded or built without names now goes when the note it was on is removed or overwritten, as in MuseScore 4. Undo restores it. Each edit now reads every line's ends once more, so it costs up to twice what it did on a score full of lines, still in step with its length.
* `AddSpanner` stores the ends of a slur or glissando the same way, so the stored spanner can differ from the one given. It names both notes of a glissando in `Glissando.from` and `Glissando.to`. It moves a slur's points to its chords' onsets and names in `Slur.endVoice` the voice of the chord the slur ends on, in place of any voice given, or clears it when that is the slur's own voice. While those chords stay, an end stays on its chord when another voice, the line's own included, later starts or ties a note there. Before, such a note could take the end. A glissando goes with its notes, so an edit that replaces or removes its end note removes the line, where the line used to move to the new chord. An end that is not stored, such as one on a rest or a slur's last end on a chord that starts at its first point, still follows the rules above.
* `SpannerSegment.startNote` and `endNote` name the notes a glissando segment joins.
* `Slur.endVoice` names the voice whose chord a slur ends on. `AddSpanner` and each edit set it when that chord is of another voice and starts after the slur's first point. MusicXML import sets it when a voice other than the slur's own starts that chord at the slur's last point, and also when the stop is written on a chord of the same staff that the slur would not otherwise end on. The slur ends on the named voice's chord while that chord starts after the slur's first note, and by the rules above otherwise. JSON writes it as an optional `endVoice` key, and a file without the key ends its slurs by the rules above. A reader before 0.5.0 ignores the key, so it ends such a slur on another chord and drops the key when it saves the file again.
* `SpannerSegment.startsOn` and `endsOn` give the events a slur, glissando or trill line starts and ends on in that bar.
* `Score.changesSince` also names the last bar of a slur, glissando or trill line over several bars when a bar from the one before its first bar to its last changes, so a layout update draws that end again. A bar named only because such a line ends in it names no other line's last bar, so an edit under a chain of lines names a handful of bars.
* **Breaking:** An edit that takes the end or the middle of a chord clears the ties out of it and removes each glissando that starts on it, as MuseScore 4 does. This holds for a shorter `SetValue` with or without a ripple, for a note or rest entered without a ripple over part of the chord, and for a rippled `Erase` or `SetValue` that removes part of another chord's time. The tie into the chord stays. A chord that loses its start to a rippled `Erase` or `SetValue` loses the ties into it and the ties out of it that end on a note. A tie out of it that ended on no note stays when the start is all the chord loses and no note of its tone follows it after the edit, as in MuseScore 4. In 0.4.0 a ripple kept the ties into and out of a note it shortened. Undo brings the ties and the lines back.
* **Breaking:** A note or rest entered with a ripple removes the ties into and out of each chord of the staves it moves that sounds across the point where it adds time, and each glissando that starts or ends on one. In 0.4.0 it kept them. MuseScore 4 takes the same from a chord that sounds across the point where it adds time. It adds the time at the cursor, and this library adds it after the rests the entry takes.
* A longer rippled `SetValue` takes no tie or glissando from the chords it makes longer. `Paste` keeps the ties and glissandi of a chord it shortens.
* `EditSession.select` moves a range start at the end of a bar to the start of the next bar, since a range starts on music as in MuseScore 4. A rippled edit no longer throws under such a range, and no longer leaves it inside an added bar or on a filler rest. `copy`, and `Erase` or `Transpose` of the session's selection, now take the range from the next bar where they took nothing or were refused. `select` also moves a range end at the start of a bar to the end of the bar before, as MuseScore 4 does, and so does every edit that leaves the session a range, `Paste` and a ripple included. A range that ends on a bar line then follows an edit the same way however it was given. A shorter bar pulls its end back with the bar. Time that a longer bar adds, or a bar inserted after the bar line, falls outside the range. Deleting the range's last bar selects nothing, and deleting the bar after the bar line keeps the range. A range over several staves follows each group of its staves that a ripple moves together, and its staves that do not move keep the range's own time. It then runs from the earliest start to the latest end, so it keeps every note that stays in place and can take in notes next to it on another staff. A rippled erase of items or change of value leaves no range only when none of its staves keeps any of its time. Such a range that starts at the end of the last bar selects nothing. The selection the session keeps is then a new object, not the one passed in.
* `Note.letRing` marks a let-ring (laissez vibrer) tie. It never lands on a note, and playback ends the note normally.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` reads a `<tied type="let-ring"/>` as `Note.letRing`, so it no longer joins a following note of its pitch. A file `scoreToMusicXml` wrote before 0.5.0 holds this mark alone on every plain tie to no note. Such a file names this library as its one `<software>` and has no `<supports>` line for the mark, so its ties stay plain and still join the tone played next across a repeat.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes a plain tie to no note as `<tied type="let-ring"/>` with a `<tie type="start"/>`, and its `<encoding>` gains a `<supports>` line for the let-ring mark. `scoreFromMusicXml` reads that pair as a plain tie, or as `Note.letRing` when a note of its pitch follows.
* A rippled `Erase` that takes only the start of a chord keeps its let-ring tie, even when a note of its tone then follows. A rippled edit that takes the end of the chord removes it. `Paste` keeps it on a note before the pasted music, and `SetTie` clears or replaces it.
* JSON gains an optional `letRing` key. The schema stays 1, older files load the same, and an older reader loads such a note untied.
* `SetLetRing(note, letRing: true)` gives a note a let-ring tie, and `letRing: false` clears it. A note with a plain tie keeps it and the edit changes nothing, as in MuseScore 4, even when that tie has no note to land on.

## 0.4.0
* **Breaking:** the sealed `Edit` gains `SetMeasureRepeat`, so a `switch` over every edit adds a case for it.
* `StaffMeasure.measureRepeat` puts a staff of a bar under a measure-repeat sign: `MeasureRepeat.oneBar` repeats the bar before, and `twoBarsFirst` with `twoBarsSecond` on the bar after repeat the two bars before. A covered staff holds one `MeasureRest` and no clef change, which the constructor checks. `copyWith` drops the sign when it writes other content. A sign stands only with its bars before it at its own length, next to its other half, and with no layout break between the halves.
* `SetMeasureRepeat(measure, bars: 1 | 2 | null, staff:)` sets or clears a sign as one undo step and is refused with `InvalidValue` where it cannot stand. After every edit, a sign that no longer stands is cleared and its bar keeps its rest. `SetMeter` with `MeterContent.keepBars` keeps each bar's signs where the bars they repeat keep theirs. Where a meter change cuts a sign or its bars apart, it writes the repeated notes out in the sign's bar first, so they go on sounding. The copy has the part of each slur, trill line, glissando and octave line on those notes that lies in the bars they repeat, and no dynamics or hairpins, so it plays at the level in force where it stands.
* `PlaybackCompiler` plays a bar under a sign as the bars it repeats, followed back through a chain of signs, on each pass of a repeat or an ending. `playingAt` reports the bar's own rest, and a `PlaybackNote.source` is the repeated event. Each replayed note takes the dynamics, slur and trill line of the note it repeats, until a dynamic or hairpin of the part written in the run of bars under signs takes over from where it stands. A hairpin that ends on the barline before the run does not take over.
* `scoreToJson` writes the sign as `measureRepeat`, and `scoreFromJson` refuses one that does not stand. JSON without the key reads as before.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes a run of signs as `<measure-style><measure-repeat type="start">` at its first bar and `type="stop"` at the bar after it, numbered by staff in a part of several staves. `scoreFromMusicXml` reads them, drops the notes of covered bars, and drops a sign of another count or one that does not stand.
* `scoreToMusicXml` writes, in each bar under a sign, the notes of the bar it plays, as MusicXML asks, so another app that ignores `<measure-style>` shows and plays them. The copy has the beams of that bar, its tuplets, and its ties to its own notes, and spells accidentals in the covered bar. It leaves out the lyrics and the slurs, hairpins and other spanners on the notes it copies. A spanner end in the covered bar lands on the copy. Reading the export back gives the same score.
* `PlaybackCompiler` trills the notes a trill line is drawn over, from the note sounding at its first point to the note sounding at its last, as the sheet draws it and MusicXML export writes it. A line whose first point fell inside a note used to start trilling at the next note, so a score played differently after a MusicXML export and import. A trilled note also trills the note it is tied to.
* `SetMeter` keeps a slur, glissando or trill line on the note it starts and ends on. A point inside a note used to move by time, onto a later part of the note that a new barline split off, so the sheet drew the line from there and the split note started to trill.
* `PlaybackCompiler` plays a tie chain as long as the one note it stands for. It used to cut the chain short by a tenth of its last note, not of the whole chain, so two tied quarters sounded longer than a half. A staccato, staccatissimo or tenuto sets the time from the note it is on to the end of the chain, so a note that `SetMeter` splits keeps the length its mark gave it.
* A ripple edit (`EnterNote`, `EnterRest`, `SetValue` or `Erase` with `Ripple.part` or `Ripple.staff`) and `SetBarLength` keep a slur, glissando or trill line on the note it starts and ends on, as `SetMeter` does. A point inside a note used to move by time, onto the tied part a barline split off or onto the next note.

## 0.3.4
* `scoreFromMusicXmlBytes` reads a `Score` from the bytes of a file, a compressed `.mxl` file or an uncompressed `.musicxml` file. It finds the score through `META-INF/container.xml`, and reads an archive without a container when it holds one `.musicxml` or `.xml` file. It decodes UTF-8, with or without a byte order mark, and UTF-16 after a byte order mark, so an app no longer decodes the text itself.
* `musicXmlFromBytes` returns the text of the document in those bytes, by the same rules, for an app that changes the document before `scoreFromMusicXml` reads it.
* `scoreFromMusicXmlBytes` reads a document of at most `maxScoreBytes`, 64 MiB by default, counted once inflated. An archive entry that declares a larger size is refused before any of it is inflated, and an entry that inflates past the size it declares is refused at that point, on the web too. Every problem with the file throws `ScoreFormatException`.
* `scoreToMxl` writes a score as a MusicXML 4.0 compressed file.
* The package depends on `archive` 4.7, through `package:archive/archive.dart` only, so it still compiles for the web.

## 0.3.3
* `ChordEvent.ornamentAbove` and `ornamentBelow` hold the accidentals of an ornament's upper and lower notes. Playback plays those notes with them, `ChordEvent.ornamentNote` names them, and each is null unless the chord has an ornament. `SetOrnament` clears them when the sign changes, and `Transpose` moves them with the notes.
* JSON writes them as `ornamentAbove` and `ornamentBelow` when set. Readers before this release skip the keys and play the key's notes.
* `scoreFromMusicXml` reads the `<accidental-mark>` after an ornament's sign, by its `placement`, and `scoreToMusicXml` writes it.

## 0.3.2
* `Score.measureView` times a voice's events and tuplets in one walk of the voice, where it walked it twice. The view it returns does not change.

## 0.3.1
* `scoreFromMusicXml` reads a note that ends nested tuplets whatever order it lists their `<tuplet type="stop">` elements in. A file that stopped the outer tuplet first was refused with "stops a tuplet that is not open".
* `scoreFromMusicXml` gives a chord the `<duration>` of its first note and ignores the durations of its other notes, as MuseScore and music21 do. A chord note with a different duration was refused with "a chord note lasts as long as its chord". Sibelius writes those durations without the notes' dots or tuplets.
